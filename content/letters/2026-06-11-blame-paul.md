Sometimes my posts are long, and in the comments someone clutches their pearls and says, "Wow… that was a lot."

Correct. That was the point. Because if Scripture has taught me anything, it's this: *God is not allergic to long teaching. Modern attention spans are.*

So if you need to stop here, go ahead, let's hear it: TL;DR. And for all you TL;DR people, here's your summary: Paul preached so long a guy died, got resurrected, and Paul kept going — therefore my long posts are not a bug, they're a feature.

For the rest of us with attention spans longer than a goldfish, I present Exhibit A: the Apostle Paul, king of long-form content. The same Paul people quote out of context to justify spiritual minimalism was a theological firehose. Romans? Long. Hebrews (yes, I said it)? Long. Ephesians? Dense. Galatians? Spicy *and* lengthy. Paul did not do three bullet points, a closing worship song, and a fog machine — done for lunch and a round of golf in the afternoon. Paul did deep theology, late nights, and zero apologies.

Which brings us to my favorite biblical moment for anyone who complains about long teaching: the night Paul preached and someone fell out a window. Acts 20:7–9, paraphrased for dramatic effect: Paul is preaching. It's nighttime. He keeps going. And keeps going. There's a young man named Eutychus sitting in a window. The hour gets late. Eutychus gets sleepy. And then he falls. Out. The window. And dies.

Let me say that again for the people in the back: *Paul preached so long a man died.* That is biblical. Now, before anyone panics — Paul goes downstairs, raises him from the dead, then goes back upstairs *and keeps talking until dawn*. You cannot make this up.

Notice what Scripture does not say. It does not say "Paul realized he should've wrapped it up," or "Paul apologized for going too long," or "Paul shortened his messages going forward." Nope. Paul said, essentially: "Good thing resurrection is part of the curriculum. Where were we?"

So why are my posts so long — and why will they stay that way? Because truth takes time. Lies are usually short. Soundbites create shallow disciples. Depth requires endurance. Jesus taught in parables. Paul taught in dissertations. The prophets wrote entire scrolls. And modern Christianity says: "Can you make it shorter? I have brunch." Hard pass.

Scripture was never meant to be skimmed. The Bible isn't a meme page. It's not a daily affirmation calendar. It's a covenant document. A legal declaration. A war manifesto. And a rescue plan. You don't speed-read a battlefield briefing.

Here's my final word for today, with love and a little fire. If my posts feel long, it's because I'm aiming for formation, not entertainment; I'm following apostles, not algorithms; and I believe people are starving for substance. Paul preached until people fell out windows. I'm just asking you to sit on the couch and read.

And hey — if you get sleepy, just don't sit near an open window.
